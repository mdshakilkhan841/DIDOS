**Annex E All 24 W13 requirements**
**W13-01**
Organization hierarchy and customer boundaries. Separate customer account/group, tenant security boundary, legal entity, institution, campus/branch, department, team, position and person. Define stable IDs, parent relationships, memberships, effective dates, status, policy inheritance and allowed aggregation. A department is not automatically a tenant. Each independent customer has a distinct boundary; group executives see only explicitly granted entities and fields. Use configurable owner/deputy roles instead of hard-coding names, brands or Daffodil authority into other customers.
**W13-02**
Login and active organization context. Specify invitation, self-registration, admin provisioning, federation discovery, verification, sign-in, step-up, account linking, recovery, suspension and sign-out. Record the exact visible fields and server-derived context in Appendix C. Prove membership independently of a claimed email domain. Role choosers only select already granted roles. Every destination independently authorizes a deep link or SSO handoff; a successful sign-in at one product is not permission in another. Where a shared enterprise IdP is selected, use separately registered application clients and protected sessions; do not share passwords, app secrets or unscoped tokens across products.
**W13-03**
Membership sessions and delegated access. Support a person with several valid memberships or roles through an explicit active context. On switching, invalidate or separate caches, pending drafts, search results, websocket subscriptions and AI context. Define joiner/mover/leaver rules, group changes, delegated authority, substitutes, temporary expert/support engagements, access reviews and expiry. Revoke sessions, refresh grants and queued privileged actions under a specified policy. Account recovery must not become a weaker administrator access path. Deny protected access at effective expiry or revocation, including already-open sessions and downstream resources. Record enforceable downstream revocation bounds; open work or connector failure cannot renew rights. Unsupported enforcement is a blocked dependency or requires an explicitly approved compensating design.
**W13-04**
Page fields forms tables and actions. Every field and action must have a stable contract, not merely a label on a mock-up. Generate a Field and Form Dictionary covering type, validation, ownership, conditional visibility, privacy, permissions, persistence, API mapping, translation, retention, audit and tests. Bind every applicable field to a screen, table, report, API or explicit non-UI purpose. Map filter/sort/export/bulk-edit behavior to the same authorization rules. Identify required database fields separately from optional visible form inputs.
**W13-05**
Complete journeys and state transitions. Define every transition by source/target state, trigger, actor, guard, required evidence, approval, side effect, timeout, escalation, retry and terminal condition. Distinguish suggestion, issued direction, approval, execution, verification, certification and publication. Prevent duplicate submission and lost concurrent edits. Include cancelled, rejected, blocked, expired, corrected and reopened states when meaningful. Do not mark a case solved because a message was generated or a task submitted. Bind approval to the exact target, action, record/evidence version, conditions and expiry; changed evidence requires a recorded disposition. Child-task closure must not silently close its parent.
**W13-06**
Dashboard evidence and decision contracts. Each widget specifies the decision it supports, cohort/scope, source, period, formula/version, denominator, unit, target, freshness limit, evidence coverage, drill-down, permitted action and accountable owner. Show missing, stale, partial, disputed and provisional data distinctly from zero or poor performance. Use a table/text alternative for charts. Compare only role-appropriate measures. Separate workflow coverage, eligible automation rate, total automation share and observed net benefit; targets are not achieved results. Preserve certified/published metric snapshots with immutable version, source lineage and linked restatements. Hours, commits or online presence alone do not establish productivity or integrity; pipeline, recognized revenue and cash remain distinct. Forecasts disclose horizon, assumptions, uncertainty and validation.
**W13-07**
Shared ownership across products. Create one versioned integration contract shared by affected products and referenced by all prompts. For each domain/field identify the authoritative writer, steward, approved commands, event publisher, consumers and certification/publication owner. A common dashboard or SaaS front end does not transfer ownership of HR, identity, academic, financial or repository records. Prevent uncontrolled duplicate masters and duplicate entry. Reconcile competing contract IDs and versions before write-back.
**W13-08**
Reliable API and event exchange. Use versioned API/schema contracts with validated tenant, actor and resource context; source record/version; effective and occurrence timestamps; classification; correlation/causation; and idempotency metadata. Specify signatures, audience checks, ordering, replay windows, retries, dead-letter ownership, corrections, retractions, deletions and reconciliation. Use an outbox/inbox or equivalent consistency mechanism where justified. A received envelope is untrusted input until the destination validates scope and authority. Failed or stale sync remains visible. Preserve canonical and native IDs. Distinguish Supported, Configurable, Adapter Required, Manual Only and Unavailable provider capabilities. Reconcile complete pagination and unknown timeout outcomes; a correlation ID is not automatically an idempotency key and transport is not guaranteed exactly once. Carry source health, as-of time and affected scope into dashboards, exports, caches and AI answers. Suppress dependent scoring or actions when required evidence is missing or stale. Restore current status only after reconciliation and revalidation, not a successful connectivity check.
**W13-09**
Field ownership and safe corrections. Enforce object, row and field permissions in services, storage, search, analytics, downloads, notifications and AI retrieval. UI masks alone provide no data security. Where scores or high-impact records exist, retain formula/policy versions and historical evidence; route corrections through an authorized independent reviewer with a reason, linked prior value, effective date and subject notification. Human decisions are separate records. Do not permit hidden score inflation or silent retrospective formula changes.
**W13-10**
Accountable work and restricted digital operations. For delivery-oriented products, record one accountable owner, executor, reviewer, deputy, deadline, dependencies, evidence, acceptance and escalation. Link approved directives and meeting decisions to work, repository issue/commit/review, release and measurable outcomes where applicable. Separate read, draft, issue, approve, execute, verify and close. Define protected production/data/access changes, temporary expert access and revocation. Virtual CTO or equivalent AI may advise and prepare work within granted authority; it cannot self-approve.
**W13-11**
Student employee and vulnerable user overlays. Activate domain-specific personas and fields only when applicable. Student services may include applicant, enrolled student, alumni, guardian/proxy, adviser, faculty, registrar, employer, mentor and scholarship/service reviewer; HR may include employee, recruiter, manager, L&D, Finance, HR and independent appeal roles. Verify relationship scope, purpose and expiry. Preserve private Diary/coaching versus deliberately submitted evidence. Do not create negative-student, loyalty or inferred political-risk scoring. Sensitive cases require proportionate, confidential evidence and human review. Distinguish support, suggestion, survey, complaint, protected disclosure, safeguarding and emergency routes. Define identified/confidential/pseudonymous/anonymous modes, anti-retaliation, small-group disclosure limits and verified relationship sharing. Do not use AI to re-identify anonymous feedback. Emergency routes must have an actual designated contact and immediate non-AI path.
**W13-12**
Controlled location and security event handling. When field verification is required, define authorized roles, working-purpose/time boundaries, necessary precision, retention, access, offline exceptions and alternative verification. Do not infer misconduct from missing GPS, a failed request or a single metric. Qualify unauthorized manipulation separately from ordinary errors. Warning or lockout rules require defined triggers, evidence, proportionality, recovery and independent review; a three-strike policy is a configurable organizational rule, not a universal default.
**W13-13**
Admin studios and configurable fields. Provide authorized editing for pages, translations, navigation, forms, custom fields, workflows, reports, policies, catalogs and organization settings. Define which changes are configuration and which need engineering. Version schemas, validations and permission dependencies; detect destructive type changes, required-field migrations and broken downstream mappings. Preview the exact role/tenant/locale/device experience. Use optimistic concurrency or equivalent conflict detection, scheduled effective dates, approval, audit and reversible release. AI-generated edits remain visible diffs. Canonical dropdown/reference data includes stable code, localized label, description, parent, source/owner, effective dates and status. An Other value requires a reason and taxonomy review rather than creating an uncontrolled duplicate category.
**W13-14**
Updates upgrades add-ons and entitlement. Distinguish code/content/configuration release, security patch, feature upgrade, connector version, database migration and commercial add-on activation. Provide a release center with compatibility, changed permissions, dependencies, entitlement, billing impact, sandbox results, staged rollout, opt-in where required, rollback/forward repair and EOL. Deploying code does not activate or bill an unpurchased module. Renew grants when an extension adds scopes. Keep customer customizations in validated configuration with an explicit inheritance/override model.
**W13-15**
Separated AI audiences and bounded tools. Keep public, authenticated personal, staff and privileged-admin knowledge and tool scopes distinct. Separate scripted assistance, generative AI, RAG, recommendation, bounded automation and agentic execution in the capability register. Define approved use, retrieved-source permissions, deletion propagation, citations/abstention, memory scope, human escalation, model/prompt versions, evaluation, budgets and kill controls. Permission in a chat does not exceed the user, organization, object or workflow grant. Map imported autonomy aliases to explicit allowed capabilities, risk limits and policy version. Student360 and SWTech use A3 differently in the reviewed sources; never infer execution authority from the same numeric label.
**W13-16**
MCP identity and protocol boundaries. Distinguish browser navigation tools, MCP client, MCP server, gateway and downstream API; a locally registered navigation function is not a production remote service. Record protocol version, transport, issuer/resource/audience, client identity, tool schema/version, purpose, read/write/destructive effects, user delegation and expiry. Use separate properly scoped upstream authorization, not token passthrough. Tool-description or manifest changes trigger review; annotations and provider branding are not authorization. Evaluate injection, confused deputy, replay, SSRF and exfiltration.
**W13-17**
Authoritative notices and human escalation. Use the in-platform task/case/notice record as the operational source. Link a memo to its actual directive or meeting ID and approved issuer when applicable; do not invent meeting references. Distinguish queued, delivered, read, acknowledged, actioned and verified closed. Define channel preferences, deduplication, quiet hours, acknowledgement/escalation rules and backups. WhatsApp/email alerts use minimal context and authenticated portal handoff for sensitive details. Resolve support through an accountable role and deputy rather than one indispensable person.
**W13-18**
Implementation status and honest public claims. Maintain independent requirement, implementation, verification and approval states. Distinguish Planned, Prototype, Simulated, Reported Built, Verified Pilot, Verified Production and Retired. Every public availability claim names feature, release/environment, supported scope, test/evidence, owner, verifier/date and expiry. A button, source claim, published preview or SSR test does not prove a database, login, API, AI service or production tenant isolation. Label synthetic data and disconnected services. Withdraw stale claims or qualify them.
**W13-19**
Real data architecture and portability. For each object identify its authoritative system, schema, migration, validation, seed/demo fixtures, live data source, attachments, indexes, retention, backup and export. Classify physical database, empty scaffold, browser memory, device-local storage, external API and proposed future schema explicitly. Separate operational records from AI/vector indexes and analytics projections; propagate correction/deletion appropriately. Do not invent a database dump when none exists. Offline mutation requires an authorized sync/conflict policy and must not cache sensitive data by default.
**W13-20**
Technology and global deployment decisions. Select technology against current source constraints, team capability, support lifecycle, security, accessibility, hosting/residency, cost, performance and portability through the DAD. Preserve working repositories and lockfiles; do not replace stacks merely to match a template. Distinguish public discovery, authenticated application and administration requirements. Record locale, currency, tax, timezone, language, low-bandwidth, assistive-technology and sector overlays. Re-open current official standards before fixing implementation versions; global readiness is not a certification claim.
**W13-21**
Release and developer handover.
